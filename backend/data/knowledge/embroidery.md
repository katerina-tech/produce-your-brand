## What the process does

Thread is stitched into the fabric by machine, following a digitised stitch file
converted from the artwork. That conversion is a skilled manual step and is
usually charged once per design.

## Where it is strong

- Garments, caps, bags and heavier textiles.
- A perceived-quality finish. Stitching reads as more premium than print on
  workwear and corporate clothing.
- Durability. Thread outlasts print through industrial laundry.
- Metallic effects, via genuine metallic thread rather than a printed imitation.

## Where it fails

- **Thin or stretchy fabric.** Stitch tension puckers it. Backing helps but does
  not fully solve it.
- **Fine detail and small text.** Below roughly 5 mm letter height, letterforms
  fill in and become unreadable. This is a hard physical limit set by needle and
  thread thickness, not a quality setting.
- **Gradients and photographs.** Thread colours are discrete; blends must be
  faked with stitch patterns.
- **Non-textiles.** Anything that will not take a needle without splitting, which
  rules out most rigid and coated goods.

## What to specify

- Stitch count, which drives price more than physical size does.
- Thread colour codes, since thread does not map cleanly onto Pantone.
- Placement and backing type, especially for caps and unlined garments.
