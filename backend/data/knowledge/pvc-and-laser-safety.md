## The rule

Reputable shops refuse PVC outright for laser work. This is a safety and
equipment matter rather than a quality preference, and no amount of extraction
makes it acceptable.

## Why

PVC (polyvinyl chloride) contains chlorine. Heating it with a laser releases
hydrogen chloride gas, which:

- is corrosive to the human airway and eyes;
- combines with moisture in the air and inside the machine to form hydrochloric
  acid, which destroys optics, rails and extraction ducting.

The damage to the machine accumulates and is expensive, which is why a supplier
who cannot verify a material composition will usually decline the job rather than
risk it.

## Practical consequence for sourcing

Many everyday products are PVC without advertising it: yoga mats, some banners,
faux leather, inflatable goods, cable sleeving. If a customer asks for engraving
on a flexible synthetic product, the material must be confirmed before quoting.

Suitable alternatives for PVC surfaces are heat transfer, screen printing with
inks formulated for vinyl, or an applied label.

## Materials often confused with PVC

- **TPE and TPU** contain no chlorine and are laser-safe in principle, though
  they still melt rather than engrave cleanly.
- **Natural rubber** is chlorine-free but produces heavy smoke and odour.
