## Is it appropriate?

Yes. This is one of the best-established pairings in the trade, and it behaves
quite differently from marking bare metal.

## Why it works

Anodising leaves a hard porous oxide layer, usually dyed. A laser at low power
bleaches or ablates that dye without cutting into the aluminium beneath. The
result is a crisp, light mark, typically white or pale grey, against the anodised
colour, and the surface stays smooth to the touch.

Because only the dye layer is affected, the process is fast, needs little power,
and does not distort thin-walled parts such as drinkware.

## What to specify

- **The anodised colour**, because contrast depends entirely on it. Black and
  dark blue give strong results. Pale grey, silver and gold give weak contrast
  and often disappoint.
- Whether the finish is genuinely anodised or merely painted or powder-coated.
  Paint burns and discolours instead of bleaching cleanly. Suppliers sometimes
  describe both as "coated", so this is worth confirming explicitly.

## Limits

- The mark colour is not selectable. It is whatever the bleached oxide layer
  looks like, so you cannot engrave a gold logo onto a black anodised bottle.
- Bare, un-anodised aluminium behaves differently. It needs a marking compound
  or a fibre laser, and results vary by alloy.
