## What the process does

A focused beam removes or alters a thin surface layer. There is no ink, no screen
and no per-unit consumable, so cost per piece barely changes between 20 units and
2000. The setup is a file, not a tool.

## Where it is the right choice

- Hard goods: metal bottles and flasks, tools, cutlery, pens.
- Wood, bamboo, cork and leather, where a burn mark reads as premium.
- Anything needing permanence. An engraved mark outlives printing and cannot peel.
- Very small batches, because there is no per-colour setup to amortise.

## Where it is the wrong choice

- **Colour.** Engraving produces contrast, not colour. A specific brand gold or
  Pantone cannot be reproduced by a laser. Anodised aluminium is the partial
  exception, covered in its own note.
- **Flexible synthetic sheet** such as yoga mats and foam. The surface melts
  rather than marking cleanly.
- **PVC in any form.** See the PVC safety note. That one is a refusal, not a
  preference.

## Practical constraints

- Marks are single-tone. Gradients and photographic detail need dithering, which
  looks coarse below roughly 30 mm.
- Curved surfaces need a rotary attachment, and not every shop has one.
- Minimum legible line weight is around 0.15 mm on metal, more on wood because
  the grain scatters the edge.
