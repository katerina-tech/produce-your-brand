## When a label beats decorating the product

- The product surface is hostile to every direct process: unknown composition,
  awkward texture, heat-sensitive, or already finished.
- The customer wants full colour and photographic detail cheaply.
- Regulatory content is required: composition, origin, care, barcodes, CE marks.
- The branding needs to change often, since a label revision costs far less than
  re-tooling a print.

## Formats

- **Self-adhesive labels**, printed on a roll. The cheapest route to full colour
  and by far the fastest to change.
- **Swing tags** on string or a loop, common for textiles and gifts.
- **Sleeves and belly bands**, which brand a product without touching it at all.
- **Printed cartons**, where the box carries the branding.

## Cost shape

Roll label minimums are high in unit terms but low in money, because the material
is cheap. A partner may quote a 1000-piece minimum and still be inexpensive in
absolute terms. Carton printing carries real tooling costs: a cutting die per
shape, plus plates per colour.

## What to confirm

- Adhesive suited to the surface. Textured, oily and low-energy plastics need a
  specific adhesive, and a generic one will lift at the edges.
- Whether application is manual or machine. Manual application on a few hundred
  pieces is a labour line item worth seeing separately.
- Substrate and lamination, which decide whether a label survives moisture and
  handling.
