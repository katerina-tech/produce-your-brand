## Quick reference

| Material | Suitable | Avoid | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PVC (mats, banners, faux leather) | heat transfer, screen printing with vinyl ink, labels | **laser (unsafe)** | Confirm the compound. Heat sensitive. |
| Natural rubber | screen printing, heat transfer | laser (smoke, odour) | Adhesion often needs a primer. |
| TPE / TPU | heat transfer, screen printing | laser (melts) | Chlorine-free, unlike PVC. |
| Cotton | screen, DTG, embroidery, transfer | laser | The most forgiving substrate there is. |
| Polyester | screen (disperse-safe ink), DTF, embroidery | DTG | Watch dye migration under heat. |
| Anodised aluminium | **laser (ideal)**, pad printing | - | Mark colour is not selectable. |
| Stainless steel | laser, pad printing | DTG | Fibre laser preferred. |
| Wood, bamboo, cork | laser, UV print | DTG | Grain affects edge crispness. |
| Glass, ceramic | laser frosting, pad printing | heat transfer | Thermal shock risk. |
| Cardboard, kraft | litho, flexo, digital, labels | laser (scorch) | Kraft shifts colour warm. |

## The two questions that resolve most cases

1. **Is the surface rigid or flexible?** Rigid tends toward engraving and pad
   printing. Flexible tends toward transfer and screen.
2. **Does the finish need to be a specific colour?** If yes, engraving is out,
   because it produces contrast rather than colour. If the colour is metallic,
   printing is out too, and foil transfer is usually the answer.

## Unknown material

If the material is genuinely unknown, no method can be confirmed. The honest
sequence is: identify the material, then order a sample, then commit. Quoting on
an assumed substrate is how projects fail at the production stage rather than at
the quotation stage.
