## Universal

- **Vector** (AI, EPS, PDF, SVG) for logos and type. Raster files cannot be
  scaled or colour-separated cleanly.
- **Outlined text.** Un-outlined fonts re-flow on the supplier machine and
  silently substitute typefaces.
- **Stated dimensions in millimetres**, plus the intended placement. "Centred,
  80 mm wide" is actionable; "our logo, fairly large" is not.
- **Colour specified as Pantone or an explicit spec**, not as a screenshot.

## By method

| Method | Needs |
|---|---|
| Laser engraving | Vector, single tone, no gradients. Minimum line about 0.15 mm on metal. |
| Screen printing | Vector separated per colour, one file per screen. Trapping for tight registration. |
| Digital printing | 300 dpi at final size minimum. CMYK, with a note where a colour is critical. |
| Pad printing | Vector, small area, two or three colours at most. |
| Heat transfer / foil | Vector. Minimum line 1 to 2 mm for weeded vinyl. |
| Embroidery | Vector for digitising. Minimum letter height about 5 mm. Thread codes, not Pantone. |
| Labels / packaging | 3 mm bleed, safe margins, plus the die line for cartons. |

## Colour expectations worth setting early

- **Metallic gold and silver cannot be printed in CMYK.** They need foil, a
  metallic screen ink, or metallic-substrate transfer.
- The same Pantone reads differently on cotton, kraft board and anodised metal.
  Substrate changes colour, always.
- Screen and print proofs are not colour-accurate. Only a physical sample on the
  real material is.
