## What the process does

Ink is deposited directly by a print head, with no screen or plate. Setup per
design is effectively zero, so cost per unit stays nearly flat regardless of run
length and regardless of how many colours the artwork contains.

## Variants worth distinguishing

- **DTG (direct-to-garment).** Ink onto textile fibre. Excellent on cotton, weak
  on polyester and coated fabrics. Needs a white underbase on dark garments,
  which stiffens the print.
- **DTF (direct-to-film).** Printed onto film, then heat pressed. More tolerant of
  mixed fabrics than DTG, and the practical route for polyester blends.
- **UV flatbed.** Ink cured by UV light onto rigid goods: acrylic, wood, coated
  metal, packaging. Can build a slight relief.

## Where it wins

- One-offs, personalisation, and any run under about 50 pieces.
- Photographic artwork, gradients and unlimited colour counts at no extra setup.
- Short lead times, because there is no tooling step.

## Where it loses

- Cost at volume. Above roughly 300 to 500 pieces, screen printing usually
  undercuts it.
- Opacity and vibrancy on dark substrates, compared with a thick screen ink.
- Metallic and fluorescent colours, which are pigment effects a CMYK head cannot
  produce. A printed gold reads as mustard brown. Genuine metallic needs foil, a
  specialty screen ink, or a metallic transfer.
