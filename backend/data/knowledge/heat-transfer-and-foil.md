## What the process does

A carrier film holding the design is pressed onto the product under heat and
pressure. The design releases from the film and bonds to the surface. Variants
include vinyl cut transfers, printed transfers, and metallic foil.

## Why it is the usual answer for metallic finishes

Genuine metallic effects are physical, not a colour mix. CMYK printing cannot
produce them. A foil transfer applies a real reflective layer, which is why a
requested gold or silver logo generally routes here rather than to printing.

## Where it fits

- Flexible synthetics that cannot be engraved: mats, bags, sports goods.
- Metallic, mirror and holographic finishes.
- Small to medium runs. Per-unit cost is higher than screen printing at volume,
  but setup is much lower.
- Customer-supplied goods, since it needs no immersion, no curing oven and no
  garment-specific tooling.

## The real constraints

- **Temperature.** PVC and other flexible synthetics soften and can gloss,
  shrink or emboss under a press. The window between adhesion and damage is
  narrower than on textiles, and the correct setting depends on the exact
  compound. This is the single most important thing to sample before committing.
- **Textured surfaces.** Foil needs contact. A pronounced grain or non-slip
  texture will break the mark into a patchy finish.
- **Durability.** A bonded film is more vulnerable to abrasion and repeated
  flexing than ink absorbed into fibre. On a surface that is knelt on and rolled
  daily, ask specifically about abrasion resistance.
- **Fine detail.** Weeded vinyl has a practical floor around 1 to 2 mm line
  weight. Thin serifs and small text lift during weeding or wash off later.
